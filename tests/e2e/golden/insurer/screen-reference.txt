Framework text
AI Risk Appetite Framework

Oct 12, 2026 · @Damian Hickey

1. Summary

This framework lets external counsel take an insurer from a general risk appetite statement to a specific appetite for AI: how much authority AI may hold, what failure it will tolerate, and what evidence it expects in return. The board makes eight decisions. Those decisions become the settings the AI Use Assessment is run against.

Item	Position
Question answered	How much authority will the insurer allow AI to hold, in its own hands and in its providers', and what evidence does it expect at each level?
Who decides	The board, on the CRO's recommendation. The appetite is the insurer's
Who runs it	External counsel leads and facilitates. Triodian supplies the structure and the technical scales
Method	Eight appetite decisions, worked through in two sessions and a challenge round
Duration	Two to three weeks, able to run alongside the first two weeks of the assessment
Inputs	The existing risk appetite statement, critical operations and their tolerances, operational risk thresholds, the AI inventory
Outputs	An AI risk appetite statement for board approval, a tolerance and trigger schedule, and a calibration set of 11 parameters
Feeds	The AI Use Assessment Framework, sections 8 and 9

Status of this document. The two source decks say evidence is measured "against the board's appetite" and that boards must oversee AI within risk appetite with defined triggers. Neither sets out how an AI appetite is built. The eight decisions, their scales, the process and the parameter set are drafted here. Section 11 lists what needs confirmation.

Standing rule. The appetite is the board's decision. What the insurer is obliged to have, and whether a stated appetite meets that obligation, are questions for counsel. Triodian supplies definitions and structure and gives no view on either.

2. Where it sits
2.1 Relation to what the insurer already has

The AI appetite is an extension of the insurer's existing risk appetite statement, not a second one. It takes its loss thresholds and critical-operation tolerances from what the board has already approved, and adds the dimensions the existing statement does not have: authority delegated to AI, and evidence expected in return.

Existing artefact	What the AI appetite takes from it	What it adds
Board risk appetite statement	Operational, conduct and outsourcing appetite positions; loss thresholds	A position on AI authority, by function and by where the AI acts
Critical operations and tolerance levels	The tolerances a failure is measured against	Which AI uses can move an operation outside tolerance
Material service provider register	The providers in scope	The authority AI may hold inside a provider
AI governance framework	Permitted uses and approvals for the insurer's own AI	The same question asked of providers and their platforms
2.2 Sequence with the assessment
Appetite sessions run in weeks 1 and 2. The assessment's mapping phase needs no calibration, so both start together.
A working appetite is agreed by the CRO before classification starts. The assessment's phase 3 uses it.
Board approval may follow the assessment. The findings are then reported against a working appetite, marked as such, and re-run if the board changes a setting.
If no appetite is set, the assessment runs on the default settings in section 9 and every output is marked uncalibrated.
2.3 What it calibrates

Each decision sets one or more parameters. Each parameter replaces a fixed rule in the AI Use Assessment Framework.

Assessment element	Framework section	Today	After calibration
Failure consequence scale, C1 to C5	8.2	Generic definitions	The insurer's own thresholds for financial loss, policyholder harm and each critical operation
Indicative rung	8.3	One rung per authority tier, plus a proposed adjustment	The board's evidence expectation for each tier and consequence
Evidence shortfall grade	9.2	A fixed matrix	The insurer's matrix, with a time limit for each grade
Not-known fields and F4 findings	6.3, 9.1	Counted and reported	Counted against a stated tolerance
Confidence in obtainability	8.4	Reported beside the finding	Tested against a minimum for higher consequences
Timely test, T5	8.4	Judged case by case	Tested against the notification times the board has set
Location	Question 4	Recorded	Each location marked accepted, escalate or not accepted
Set selection	2.2	Five criteria	A sixth: arrangements likely to sit above the authority ceiling
2.4 One new result in the assessment

Calibration adds a result the uncalibrated assessment cannot give. An entry whose authority tier is higher than the ceiling the board set for that function is flagged above ceiling. This is separate from the evidence finding. An AI use can have all the evidence expected and still hold more authority than the board has accepted.

2.5 The rungs after calibration
Position	Meaning	Set by
Expected rung	The evidence the board expects for this authority and consequence. Replaces the indicative rung	The board, through decision 3
Required rung	The evidence the insurer can reasonably require at this boundary	The insurer and counsel
Obtainable rung	The evidence that can in fact be produced and independently checked	Triodian, by test

The board's expectation is an input to counsel's determination. It does not replace it. Where counsel finds the insurer cannot require what the board expects, that difference is itself reported.

2.6 How this framework reads beside ISO vocabulary

The framework uses its own scales. Where its outputs enter an insurer's risk framework or AI management system, they can be read against ISO terms as follows.

Risk criteria. The eight decisions set the insurer's risk criteria for AI: the terms against which the significance of a result is judged. That is what ISO 31000:2018 clause 6.3.4 (defining risk criteria) and ISO/IEC 42001:2023 clause 6.1.2 ask an organisation to establish.

Term here	ISO Guide 73:2009 (now ISO 31073:2022)	How the framework uses it
AI risk appetite	Risk appetite: the amount and type of risk that an organisation is willing to pursue or retain	The eight decisions together, recorded in the AI risk appetite statement
Tolerance level	Risk tolerance: an organisation's or stakeholder's readiness to bear the risk after risk treatment in order to achieve its objectives	Decision 2 uses the narrower operational-risk sense: the most disruption to a critical operation the board accepts. Decisions 3 to 5 record what the board is ready to bear while evidence is short or facts are not known
The eight decisions	Risk criteria: the terms of reference against which the significance of a risk is evaluated	Decisions 1 to 8, held as the calibration set
Trigger	No term of its own. A threshold on a monitoring measure, under ISO 31000:2018 clause 6.6 (monitoring and review)	Decision 7: a defined event reported to the insurer within a set time

Uncertainty. Decision 5 (unknowns) and check K10 (reachable) record what is not known and whether an expectation can be met at all. ISO 31000:2018 clause 6.4.3 (risk analysis) asks for uncertainties to be considered; here they are counted and given a limit, not estimated.

Likelihood. The suite does not assess likelihood. A grade here is the size of the gap between the evidence expected and the evidence obtainable, read against the consequence band. It is not a risk rating in the sense of ISO 31000:2018, and nothing in the outputs should be read as one.

Privilege. The sessions and the drafts are scoped through counsel, on the position counsel determines, in the same way as the assessment.

4. The eight appetite decisions

Each decision is one question to the board, answered on a fixed scale, recorded with an owner, and turned into one or more parameters.

#	Decision	Question to the board	Answered as	Parameter
1	Authority	How much authority may AI hold in each function, in our own hands, in a provider's and in a platform behind one?	A ceiling tier, A1 to A6, for each function and location in the chain	P1
2	Consequence thresholds	At what point does a failure count as a financial loss, as policyholder harm, and as disruption of a critical operation?	A threshold for each of C3, C4 and C5	P2
3	Evidence expectation	For each level of authority and consequence, what evidence do we expect to hold?	A rung, E0 to E6, for each tier and consequence	P3
4	Shortfall tolerance	When the evidence falls short, how serious is it and how long may it stand?	A grade matrix and a time limit for each grade	P4, P5
5	Unknowns	How much may we not know about AI in our chain?	A limit on not-known facts and a minimum confidence, by consequence	P6, P7
6	Location and concentration	Where may AI that acts for us run, and how much may depend on one platform?	Each location marked accepted, escalate or not accepted; a concentration limit	P8, P9
7	Triggers	What events must reach us, and how fast?	A list of triggers, each with a measure, a threshold and a time	P10
8	Change and review	What changes bring an arrangement back for approval, and how often is the appetite reviewed?	A list of change events and a review interval	P11

Decisions 1 to 3 are taken in session 1. Decisions 4 to 8 are taken in session 2.

Scales. The authority tiers (A1 to A6), consequence levels (C1 to C5) and evidence rungs (E0 to E6) are those defined in the AI Use Assessment Framework, sections 8.1 to 8.3. They are not redefined here.

Functions. The insurer's own function list is used. Where it has none suited to the purpose, the starting list is: underwriting and pricing; claims; fraud and investigations; policy administration; complaints and disputes; customer communications; finance and reserving; legal and compliance; technology operations.

5. Decisions 1 to 4

Every starting position below is a place to begin the discussion. It is not a recommendation, and the board may set any position it can justify.

5.1 Decision 1: authority

Purpose. To state the most authority AI may hold in each function, depending on whose hands it is in.

How it is recorded. One ceiling tier per cell, with any condition written beside it.

Function	Own staff	Inside a provider	Platform behind a provider
Underwriting and pricing
Claims
Fraud and investigations
Policy administration
Complaints and disputes
Customer communications
Finance and reserving
Legal and compliance
Technology operations

Prompts for the session.

Where does a named person sign off today? Would we accept that sign-off being removed?
Would we accept a provider's AI declining or paying a claim with no person involved? Up to what amount?
Is our position different when the AI is ours and when it is a provider's? Why?
Is there any function where AI may assist but must never decide?
Who may approve a use above the ceiling, and for how long?

Conditions. A ceiling may carry a limit, for example "A4 up to a stated amount per transaction" or "A3 for triage only". Each condition must be something the assessment can test from documents.

Starting position. The current state. The session begins from where a person signs off today in each function, and asks whether the board accepts that as the ceiling.

5.2 Decision 2: consequence thresholds

Purpose. To replace the generic consequence definitions with the insurer's own, so two classifiers reach the same level.

Level	Generic definition	What the insurer sets	Usual source
C1, immaterial	No effect outside the team using it	Nothing. It is whatever falls below C2	n/a
C2, contract breach	A breach of an agreement term	Whether any class of breach is treated as higher	Outsourcing policy
C3, financial loss	Loss above the reporting threshold	An amount for a single event and for a period	Operational risk incident thresholds
C4, policyholder harm	A policyholder wrongly declined, delayed, charged or paid	The number affected, and the kinds of harm, at which this level applies	Conduct and breach reporting definitions
C5, critical-operation disruption	A critical operation outside tolerance	For each critical operation in scope, the tolerance already approved	Critical operations register

Prompts.

Do we already have these thresholds for operational incidents? If so, we use them unchanged.
Does one wrongly declined claim count as policyholder harm, or does it take a pattern?
Which critical operations could an AI failure, ours or a provider's, move outside tolerance?

Starting position. The insurer's existing operational risk and conduct thresholds, unchanged. A new threshold is set only where none exists. Where one is needed, the Independent Reviewer advises the CRO on the figure. The Independent Reviewer advises on this decision only.

5.3 Decision 3: evidence expectation

Purpose. To state, for each combination of authority and consequence, the evidence the board expects to hold. This is the centre of the calibration.

How it is recorded. One rung per cell.

Authority	C1	C2	C3	C4	C5
A1, productivity
A2, analytical
A3, decision
A4, transactional
A5, agentic
A6, actuation

Starting position. The current state. Before the session, each cell is filled with the evidence the insurer holds today for uses of that kind, taken from the inputs in section 7.1 and from the assessment's register where mapping has started. Cells with no current use are left empty. The session asks whether the board accepts what it holds today as its expectation. A reference table drawn from the assessment framework is in Appendix A. It is shown only if the insurer asks for it.

Prompts.

For a provider's AI that decides claims, is a signed statement each quarter enough, or do we expect to be told when a limit is crossed?
At what point do we expect to audit the aggregate record ourselves?
Is there any use for which we expect a control that does not depend on the AI behaving as instructed?
What would we want to hold if a regulator or a trustee asked tomorrow?

Rule. Moving right or down the table, the rung may stay level or rise. It may not fall.

Gap	C1 or C2	C3	C4 or C5

6. Decisions 5 to 8
6.1 Decision 5: unknowns

Purpose. To state how much the insurer may not know about AI in its chain. The assessment approaches no provider, so some facts will be unknown. This decision says how many, and for how long.

Consequence of the arrangement	Not-known facts allowed per entry	F4 finding allowed to stand	Minimum confidence in the obtainable rung
C1 or C2	No limit	Until the next annual review	Low
C3	A stated number	Until the next contracting point	Medium
C4 or C5	None among the facts that decide the authority tier	Until a stated date; then the provider is asked	Medium

Starting position. The table above, with the stated number and date left for the session.

Prompts.

If we cannot tell from our documents whether a claims manager uses AI to decide, how long are we content not to know?
At what point do we ask the provider directly, accepting that this takes the work beyond a desk assessment?
6.2 Decision 6: location and concentration

Purpose. To state where AI acting for the insurer may run, and how much may rest on one platform.

Location. Each jurisdiction that appears in the register is marked once.

Marking	Meaning in the assessment
Accepted	Recorded. No further step
Escalate	A question to counsel is raised for every entry in that location
Not accepted	The entry is flagged in the same way as an above-ceiling result

The marking is made separately for where the provider is established and where the AI runs. Whether a location limits audit rights, data access or enforceability is counsel's question.

Concentration. One limit is set: the largest share of critical operations that may depend on a single platform or model provider. The register's platform field makes this countable across the set.

Prompts.

Do we know which model provider sits behind each of our material providers?
If one platform failed or changed its terms, how many critical operations would be affected?
Is our position on offshore AI the same as our existing position on offshoring generally?

Starting position. The insurer's existing offshoring position for location. No starting position for concentration; most insurers will not yet have counted it.

6.3 Decision 7: triggers

Purpose. To define the events that must reach the insurer, and how fast. APRA's letter, as quoted in the counsel briefing, expects monitoring with "clearly defined triggers".

Ref	Trigger	Measure	Threshold	Notify within	Notified to
TR1	Authority change	A provider's AI moves to a higher tier	Any move to A3 or above
TR2	Evidence lapse	An expected evidence item is not received	Days past due
TR3	Tolerance signal	An aggregate measure approaches or crosses a declared tolerance	Set per measure
TR4	AI incident	An event involving AI that could disrupt a critical operation	Any
TR5	Other AI incident	Any other defined AI event at a provider	As defined
TR6	Model or platform change	The provider changes the model or platform behind the service	Any, for A3 or above
TR7	Sub-contractor change	A new party enters the chain	Any, for a critical operation
TR8	Location change	The AI runs, or the provider operates, from a new location	Any

Rule for timing. Each time must leave the insurer able to assess the event and meet its own notification obligations. The counsel briefing's example wording used 12 hours for an event that could disrupt a critical operation and 48 hours for other triggers. Those figures are examples; counsel settles the timing.

Starting position. The eight triggers above, with thresholds and times left for the session.

6.4 Decision 8: change and review

Purpose. To state what brings an arrangement, or the appetite itself, back for decision.

Event	What is revisited
A new arrangement where AI is at A3 or above	That arrangement is assessed before it starts
TR1, TR6, TR7 or TR8 fires	The register entry is reclassified and the finding re-set
An arrangement reaches its contracting point	Its evidence terms are reviewed against the expectation
A material AI incident, at the insurer or in its chain	Decisions 1, 3 and 7
New regulatory guidance on AI or outsourcing	All eight, on counsel's advice
The interval set by the board has passed	All eight

Starting position. Review at least annually, in the same cycle as the main risk appetite statement.

7.3 Running a session
One decision at a time, in order. Later decisions depend on earlier ones.
Start from what is already decided. Where an existing document answers a question, the session confirms it and moves on.
Use a real arrangement. Each decision is tested against one of the arrangements in the assessment's set before it is recorded.
Record positions, not discussion. Each cell has a value, an owner and, where it applies, a condition.
Park the legal questions. Anything that turns on what the insurer is obliged to do is logged for counsel and not settled in the room.
Leave nothing blank. A cell the room cannot fill is recorded as "not set", with who will set it and by when.
Positions come from the insurer. On decisions 1 and 3, only the CRO or the insurer's risk function proposes a position. Counsel and Triodian explain, answer questions and record. Neither proposes or recommends where a ceiling or an expectation should be set.
The Independent Reviewer speaks to decision 2 only. It advises on thresholds and leaves the room for the other decisions.

9.3 Rules
A change of version re-runs the rules. Expected rungs, flags, grades and due dates are recalculated. Facts, classifications and counsel's determinations are not touched.
The difference is reported. When a version changes, the assessment lists every entry whose result changed and why.
Partial sets are allowed. A parameter left as not set falls back to its default, and the outputs say which ones did.
The set carries no client facts. It holds positions and thresholds only, so it can be shared with the assessment team without the session record.
10. Consistency checks and governance
10.1 Checks before the working appetite is issued

Run by Triodian on the recorded positions. A failed check goes back to the CRO; Triodian does not resolve it.

#	Check	Fails when
K1	Complete	Any cell is blank and not recorded as "not set" with an owner and date
K2	Ordered	In the expectation table, a rung falls when moving to higher authority or higher consequence
K3	Grade ordered	In the grade matrix, the grade falls as the gap or the consequence grows
K4	Consistent with existing thresholds	A consequence threshold differs from the insurer's existing one without a recorded reason
K5	Consistent with existing authority	A ceiling is higher than the delegated authority or the AI policy currently allows for that function
K6	Provider against own	A ceiling inside a provider is higher than for the insurer's own staff in the same function, without a recorded reason
K7	Testable	A condition on a ceiling cannot be tested from documents
K8	Owned	A position or a measure has no accountable person
K9	Timed	A trigger has no time, or a time longer than the insurer's own notification procedure can absorb
K10	Reachable	The expectation for the most common authority and consequence pair in the set is above anything currently obtained anywhere in the set

K10 is not a failure of logic. It tells the board that its expectation will show as a gap on most arrangements from the first day, so that position is taken knowingly.

11. Assumptions and open items
11.1 Assumptions
The insurer has a current board risk appetite statement and a critical operations register with tolerances. Without them, decision 2 has nothing to draw on and the work takes longer.
Two sessions of two hours are enough. This is an estimate and has not been tested.
The CRO can agree a working appetite without board approval.
The assessment's scales (A1 to A6, C1 to C5, E0 to E6) are settled before session 1.
